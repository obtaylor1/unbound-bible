# Release usability test guide

## Evidence status

**Zero human sessions have been completed.** Automated agent simulation is useful for finding regressions, but is not usability evidence and must not be reported as participant research.

Recruit and document at least one participant aged 13–17 with guardian or responsible-organization consent, and at least one participant aged 65+. Do not collect sensitive personal data. Use a staging account with no personal notes or reading history.

## Moderator opening

“Thank you for helping us test the product, not yourself. There are no wrong answers. Please think aloud: say what you expect, notice, and find confusing. I may ask neutral questions, but I will not teach you how to complete a task.”

Use neutral prompts only: “What are you looking for now?”, “What would you expect that to do?”, “Tell me what you notice.” Do not say “try the book picker” or otherwise direct the participant. Stop if the participant wishes, becomes distressed, discloses sensitive information, or the session would require collecting it.

## Moderator scripts

### Ages 13–17

Before beginning, confirm guardian or responsible-organization consent and the participant’s assent. Explain that no name, email, school, religious affiliation, or other sensitive information is needed; they can skip any question and stop at any time. Avoid asking about personal belief, trauma, or family circumstances. Offer a guardian/organization contact and use a quiet, accessible setting.

### Ages 65+

Ask what accessibility accommodations would help (larger display, reduced glare, assistive technology, extra time, or breaks). Do not assume prior smartphone or Bible-app experience. Permit the participant to use their usual device settings and pace.

## Tasks (use the same eight for every participant)

1. Open Genesis chapter 1 and read the first verse.
2. Make the text easier to read by changing text size or theme.
3. Find a different book and chapter.
4. Change between the composite English edition and KJV.
5. Explain, in your own words, what the composite edition and its current source information mean.
6. Find commentary for a verse.
7. Highlight or bookmark a verse, then add or find a note.
8. Recover from a sign-in, network, or unavailable-text error and continue reading another available book.

After each task, record completion (complete / partial / not complete), time, errors, and help given. Ask for 1–5 ease and confidence ratings. After tasks 4–6, ask a comprehension question: “What do you think this label/source information tells you?” Do not correct the answer until after the rating.

## Findings and privacy

Take only de-identified notes. Assign a random participant code, keep consent records separately from observations, remove direct quotations that identify someone, and restrict access to the research team. Report aggregates, not identities.

Rate findings: **1 Critical**—harm, privacy risk, or task cannot be completed; **2 Serious**—major delay/error with no reliable recovery; **3 Moderate**—clear friction or repeated confusion; **4 Minor**—cosmetic or isolated friction.
